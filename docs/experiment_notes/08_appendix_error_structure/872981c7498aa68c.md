# VisualTorque — Weight-Shortcut Error Decomposition

This confirmatory experiment asks why a model follows the heavier side when exact torque predicts the opposite answer. It separates two explanations that the previous targeted intervention could not distinguish: the model may overuse weight, or it may underuse distance.

Every family contains a four-stack base scene and five natural counterfactual sources. All stacks stay inside Dataset v1's unit and distance ranges. Every source preserves the base scene's exact net torque and correct outcome. Weight sources keep all distances fixed while neutralizing, aligning, or amplifying the weight-only cue. Distance sources keep all weights fixed while strengthening or weakening the correct distance-only cue. Thus an intervention cannot help by importing a different answer or a larger physical margin.

At each previously frozen site, all causal directions within a comparison are norm matched. The primary analysis asks whether weight neutralization rescues existing weight-shortcut errors and weight amplification induces them in initially correct cases; an analogous bidirectional test evaluates distance strengthening and weakening. The notebook classifies the result as overweighting, underuse of distance, joint competition, underpowered, or unsupported. Qwen 9B and Qwen 27B are the confirmatory models; Gemma 12B is retained as an optional boundary audit.
