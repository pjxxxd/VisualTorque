# VisualTorque — Qwen-27B Weight-Route Competence-Gating Audit

The paired mapping-by-side experiment showed that the weight intervention was
reliable in only one of four cells. This notebook asks whether that
conditionality is predictable from the model's behavior *before* any internal
intervention.

For every physical family, target context, mirrored side, and answer mapping,
we measure how confidently the untouched model handles both the original scene
and its natural weight-changed counterpart. We then compare three explanations
of intervention strength:

1. one invariant average effect;
2. fixed dependence on physical side and answer mapping;
3. the same factorial structure plus pre-intervention scene competence.

The primary outcome is the real shared weight-state effect minus its equal-norm
random control. Frozen-route attenuation and specificity are secondary. Entire
physical families are held out during prediction, and all uncertainty is
clustered by family. No model weights are loaded and no examples are selected
using intervention success.

This is an analysis of whether pre-intervention competence *predicts* causal
route expression. It does not prove that competence is itself a causal gate.
