# VisualTorque — Qwen-27B Cross-Context Factor Equivalence and Product Interaction

The earlier graded composition experiment moved one factor change into three
target contexts and did not confirm a product-law response.  That design could
not tell whether the transferred state was a reusable factor signal or a
source-specific mixture of factor, torque, and answer information.

This notebook runs a stricter crossed test.  Every family contains three
physically identical factor changes obtained from different source contexts
and three target contexts that retain different values of the complementary
factor.  For weight, the same weight change is crossed with source and target
distances; for distance, the same distance change is crossed with source and
target weights.  The primary comparison asks whether the causal effect follows
the complementary factor in the target scene, as recombination predicts, or
the complementary factor and answer in the source scene, as holistic carryover
predicts.

The three source changes are also decomposed into their common component and
source-specific residuals.  A strong result requires the common component to
reproduce the natural counterfactual dose response, the source-specific
residuals to be weaker, and the frozen L49/L57 route to mediate the response.
This supports a context-general, causally reusable factor signal; it does not
claim that individual neurons encode a mathematically independent variable.

Candidate families are constructed without model outputs.  A preregistered
endpoint-competence screen then requires at least two target base/counterfactual
pairs to be solved correctly with an observable output contrast.  Source
endpoints are deliberately not filtered by their final answers: they provide
candidate internal factor changes, and filtering them on behavior would bias
the mechanism test.  Intervention outcomes are never used for selection.  All
model sites, masks, thresholds, controls, and decision rules are frozen before
the confirmatory capture.
