# VisualTorque — Qwen3.5-35B-A3B BF16 Net-Torque Reciprocal Causal Test

The preceding 900-scene replication found that signed net torque is often recoverable even when the official non-quantized 35B checkpoint gives the wrong tipping direction. This notebook expands the behavioral screen from the earlier 120-error diagnostic subset to all 324 untouched probe-test scenes, then asks where correctly available net-torque information stops controlling the answer.

Before any intervention, it keeps left-versus-right outcome errors whose net-torque sign is correct under the held-out readout, records direct net-torque answers in a new resumable screen, and pairs each with a physically similar correct scene under strict outcome, cue-pattern, and distance constraints. Both scenes are replayed under two A/B/C mappings that swap the letters assigned to the correct and incorrect physical sides. Direct net-torque correctness is reported as a prespecified subgroup rather than used to discard most of the causal support.

Three model-specific directions are learned without target interventions: the frozen L20 net-torque readout, a reusable physical left/right direction at L30, and the current prompt's answer-code transition at L34. Correct directions, reversed directions, stale mappings, and equal-norm random controls are tested at three strengths. The primary evidence is reciprocal: a valid fracture site should both repair the frozen errors and induce the predicted error when the same route is reversed in matched correct scenes.

The three sites were fixed before this run: L20 is the 35B net-torque layer selected on probe_train/probe_dev; L30 and L34 are normalized-depth transfers of the previously localized physical-semantic and response-code stages. Target scenes are probe_test only. Run in a fresh session on one CUDA GPU with at least 90 GiB. All causal outputs are resumable per matched pair.
