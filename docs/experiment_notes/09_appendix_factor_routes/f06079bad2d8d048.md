# VisualTorque — Qwen-27B Factor Path across Tokens and Components

The previous experiment found reliable, background-appropriate answer changes
after decision-neutral weight and distance interventions, but neither the
canonical L49 axis nor pilot-selected low-dimensional final-token subspaces
mediated those effects.  This notebook tests the next, narrower hypothesis:
the factor effect may travel through a sparse route defined by a **layer,
token region, and decoder component**, rather than through a compact final-token
linear bottleneck.

The experiment creates model-blind decision-neutral families that exclude all
scenes in the immediately preceding factor-recombination manifest.  It first
uses a deterministic multi-start search for a globally scene-disjoint support;
if the finite factorial grid cannot supply one, weight and distance retain
separate within-factor scene-disjoint supports and any cross-factor overlap is
recorded explicitly.  On pilot
families only, it scans L49/L53/L57/L61, question/options/instruction/final tokens,
and attention/MLP/decoder outputs.  At each site it restores the additive run's
activation to its clean-target value and compares the resulting answer change
with an equal-norm random removal at the identical site.  Two sites at distinct
layers are selected per factor and frozen.

Confirmation evaluates the primary site, secondary site, their joint
restoration, and an equal-norm joint random control on untouched families.
A positive route requires a fresh-support factor effect, a practically
meaningful joint attenuation, superiority over the random control, and effects
in both target backgrounds.  Failure rules out this frozen sparse route, not
all distributed, nonlinear, or token-wide mechanisms.
